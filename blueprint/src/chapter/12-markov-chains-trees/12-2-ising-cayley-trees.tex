\section{The Ising model on Cayley trees}

\begin{definition}[Georgii (12.19)--(12.24)]
    \label{def:ict-transfer}
    \uses{def:tr-transfer, def:tr-boundary-law, thm:tr-12-12}
    \lean{MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.isingTransfer, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.isTransferFamily_isingTransfer, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.isingBoundaryVec, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.isBoundaryLaw_isingBoundaryVec_iff, Real.logCoshRatio, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.isingChain, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.boundaryLawTransition_isingBoundaryVec, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.integral_spin_isingChain}
    \leanok

    On a Cayley tree of degree $d + 1$ the Ising transfer family is a transfer family, and the
    vectors $\ell_t$ are boundary laws exactly when $t$ solves $t = h + d\varphi_J(t)$, where
    $\varphi_J = \operatorname{artanh}(\tanh J \tanh(\cdot))$ (12.23). Each solution gives a
    completely homogeneous Markov chain with transition matrix $P_t$ and magnetization (12.25).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Georgii (12.26)--(12.30)]
    \label{thm:ict-12-27}
    \uses{def:ict-transfer}
    \lean{Real.treeCriticalPoint, Real.deriv_treeField_treeCriticalPoint, Real.treeCriticalField, Real.isGreatest_treeCriticalField, Real.existsUnique_treeField_eq_of_le_or_lt, Real.exists_eq_pair_treeField_eq_of_abs_eq, Real.exists_eq_insert_treeField_eq_of_abs_lt, Real.treeCriticalField_eq, Real.treeCriticalField_eq_zero_iff, Real.criticalCoupling}
    \leanok

    Write $w = \tanh J$ and $h(J,d) = \max_{t \geq 0}[d\varphi_J(t) - t]$. The equation
    $t = h + d\varphi_J(t)$ has one solution if $dw \leq 1$ or $h(J,d) < \abs h$, two if
    $\abs h = h(J,d) > 0$, and three if $\abs h < h(J,d)$; and $h(J,d) = 0$ iff $dw \leq 1$, with
    the closed form (12.30) otherwise.
\end{theorem}

    If $dw \leq 1$ or $\abs h > h(J,d)$ then $\mathcal G$ is a singleton. If $dw > 1$ and
    $\abs h \leq h(J,d)$ there are two distinct completely homogeneous chains in $\mathcal G$, and
    three when $\abs h < h(J,d)$; at $h = -h(J,d)$ the upper phase has magnetization
    $\sqrt{(d-w)(d-\bar w)}/(d-1)$. (b), steps 3--4: $\mu_-$ and $\mu_+$ are extreme in
    $\mathcal G(J,h)$ and in $\mathcal G_{I(B)}(J,h)$ --- the ratios $r_{\Lambda,\zeta}(\mu)$ of
    Georgii's proof are monotone in the boundary law, so a boundary law dominating (dominated by)
    that of every extreme Gibbs measure, through Theorem (7.26) as a comparison principle, has an
    extreme measure; and the family $(u_{d(r,i)}, v_{d(r,i)})$ built from a non-fixed orbit of
    $h + d\varphi_J$ between two fixed points is a boundary law (the converse of (12.32)), giving
    infinitely many inhomogeneous Markov chains in $\mathcal G(J,h)$ that are not completely
    homogeneous. Not proved: that $\operatorname{ex}\mathcal G(J,h) \setminus
    \operatorname{ex}\mathcal G_{I(B)}(J,h)$ is infinite (the linear independence of the $\nu_t$
    and the transitivity of the automorphism group of the Cayley tree on oriented bonds, not in
    the tree; with $I(B)$ trivial the clause would be false) and step 5, the third extreme element
    $\mu_\#$ of $\mathcal G_{I(B)}$ (the mixing estimate under $I(B,n)$).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    Every normalised boundary law has its parameters bounded by solutions of the fixed-point
    equation, so uniqueness of the solution forces the boundary law; the extreme decomposition then
    gives $\mathcal G = \{\mu_*\}$.
\end{proof}

    \label{prop:ict-12-34}
    \uses{def:ict-transfer, cor:tr-12-18}
    \lean{MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.isingAltLaw, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.isBoundaryLaw_isingAltLaw_iff, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.isingAltChain, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.isGibbsMeasure_isingAltChain, Real.treeRecursion₂, Real.isFixedPt_treeRecursion₂_iff, Real.existsUnique_isFixedPt_treeRecursion₂, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.exists_ne_isGibbsMeasure_of_isFixedPt_treeRecursion₂, SimpleGraph.IsCayleyTree.exists_bool_coloring, Real.antiferroCriticalField, Real.antiferroCriticalField_eq, Real.antiferroCriticalField_pos, Real.exists_isFixedPt_treeRecursion₂_ne, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.exists_ne_isGibbsMeasure_of_abs_lt_antiferroCriticalField, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.exists_ne_isGibbsMeasure_isingSpecification_antiferro, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.exists_ne_isGibbsMeasure_isingSpecification_reducedWordTree_antiferro}
    \leanok

    Alternating boundary laws along the bipartition of the tree are boundary laws exactly at the
    fixed points of $\psi = (h + d\varphi_J)^{\circ 2}$, which is increasing for either sign of
    $J$ and has a unique fixed point when $d\abs w \leq 1$. A fixed point of $\psi$ that does not
    solve (12.22) gives two distinct Gibbs measures, the antiferromagnetic phase transition; such a
    fixed point exists for $J < -J(d)$ and $\abs h < \hat h(J,d) = t_{-J,d} - d\varphi_J(t_{-J,d})$,
    since then the unique solution $t_*$ of (12.22) has $(d\varphi_J'(t_*))^2 > 1$ by (12.29), so
    $\psi - \mathrm{id}$ changes sign to the right of $t_*$. Georgii's $I(J,d) = ]-\hat h, \hat h[$
    is stated in the book as suggested by numerical calculation; the inclusion $\supseteq$ is what
    is proved, witnessed on $\mathcal{CT}(2)$ at $J = -\operatorname{artanh}(3/4)$, $h = 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Georgii (12.7), (12.9): the tree specification is Gibbsian]
    \label{thm:ict-identification}
    \uses{def:ict-transfer, def:pot-gibbs-spec}
    \lean{MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.hamiltonian_isingPotential, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.transferWeight_isingTransfer_eq, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.isingTreeSpecification_eq_isingSpecification, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.exists_G_isingSpecification_eq_singleton, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.exists_ne_isGibbsMeasure_isingSpecification}
    \leanok

    On a Cayley tree the specification built from the Ising transfer family is the Gibbsian
    specification of the Ising potential, so the results of this section are statements about
    $\mathcal G(\Phi^{J,h})$ as Georgii intends.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    The Hamiltonian is $-J\sum_{b}\sigma_i\sigma_j - h\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\sigma_i$, and each site of
    $\Lambda$ meets exactly $d+1$ bonds, so the bond product over the bonds meeting $\Lambda$ is the
    Boltzmann factor times a weight depending only on the configuration off $\Lambda$; such a factor
    does not change the $\lambda$-specification.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The phase transition on $\mathcal{CT}(2)$]
    \label{cor:ict-witness}
    \uses{thm:ict-identification, thm:ict-12-31}
    \lean{SimpleGraph.reducedWordTree, SimpleGraph.isCayleyTree_reducedWordTree, SimpleGraph.isRegularOfDegree_reducedWordTree, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Tree.exists_ne_isGibbsMeasure_isingSpecification_reducedWordTree}
    \leanok

    The reduced words over $d+1$ letters form a tree in which every vertex has degree $d+1$, so
    $\mathcal{CT}(d)$ exists for every $d$. At $d = 2$, $J = \operatorname{artanh}(3/4)$ and $h = 0$
    the Ising model on it has two distinct Gibbs measures.
\end{corollary}
