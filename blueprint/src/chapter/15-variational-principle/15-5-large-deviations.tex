    \leanok

    Let $G$ be a countable linearly ordered abelian group acting measurably on $\Omega$
    preserving an ergodic probability measure $\mu$, $X : \Omega \to E$ measurable with $E$
    finite, and $p_\Lambda(\omega) = \mu(X_i = X_i(\omega)\ \forall i \in \Lambda)$ the block
    probabilities. The entropy rate $h$ is the infimum over finite past windows $W \subset
    \{i < 0\}$ of $\int g_W\,d\mu$, $g_W = -\log \mu(X_0 = X_0(\omega) \mid X_W)$. Along every
    F\o lner net $(\Lambda_k)$, $\int \bigl| -|\Lambda_k|^{-1}\log p_{\Lambda_k} - h \bigr|\,d\mu
    \to 0$; on $\mathbb Z^d$ with the lexicographic order this holds along any sequence of cubes
    with side lengths tending to infinity. This is the $L^1$ form Georgii uses in (15.47).
    For $\mu \in \mathcal P_\Theta$ on $E^{\mathbb Z^d}$ with $E$ finite, the entropy rate of
    the origin given the lexicographic past and the specific entropy of \S15.2 relative to the
    uniform distribution are related by $\mathfrak k(\mu) = h - \log|E|$, without ergodicity;
    hence, for ergodic $\mu$ and $f_\Lambda = |E|^{|\Lambda|}\mu(\sigma_\Lambda = \cdot)$,
    Georgii's displayed statement $\mu\bigl(\bigl||\Lambda_n|^{-1}\log f_{\Lambda_n} +
    \mathfrak k(\mu)\bigr|\bigr) \to 0$ along cubes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    The exact chain rule $-\log p_\Lambda(\omega) = \sum_{i \in \Lambda} g_{W_i(\Lambda)}(i +
    \omega)$ over the past windows $W_i(\Lambda) = (\Lambda \cap \{j < i\}) - i$; for $W' \subseteq
    W$, $\int e^{g_W - g_{W'}} \leq 1$, and $\int e^u \leq 1$ on a probability space gives
    $\int|u| \leq 2\delta + (1 + 2/\delta)(-\int u)$, so the $g_W$ are uniformly $L^1$-close to
    $g_{W_0}$ for all $W \supseteq W_0$ once $\int g_{W_0}$ is within $\varepsilon$ of $h$;
    the F\o lner property makes the sites whose window misses $W_0$ negligible, and the ergodic
    theorem in $L^1$ replaces $|\Lambda_k|^{-1}\sum_i g_{W_0}(i + \omega)$ by $\int g_{W_0}$.
    Georgii's $f_\Lambda$ is identified with the Radon--Nikodym derivative of $\mu|_{\mathcal F_\Lambda}$
    against the uniform product, so his display is stated with that derivative as well.
    The identification with $\mathfrak k(\mu)$ compares the two limits along the cubes
    $[-n,0]^d$, using Shannon's formula $\mathcal H_\Lambda(\mu \mid \bar\lambda^\Lambda) =
    -\int \log \mu(\sigma_\Lambda = \sigma_\Lambda(\omega))\,d\mu - |\Lambda|\log|E|$
    for the uniform $\bar\lambda$; no upward martingale theorem is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Choquet barycentres, Phelps Prop.\ 1.2 and Lemma 9.7, the input of Step 3 of (15.46)]
    \label{thm:ld-barycenter}
    \lean{MeasureTheory.IsBarycenter, MeasureTheory.isBarycenter_dirac, MeasureTheory.IsBarycenter.smul_add_smul, MeasureTheory.IsBarycenter.unique, MeasureTheory.IsBarycenter.integral_continuousAffineMap, MeasureTheory.exists_isBarycenter_of_isCompact, MeasureTheory.existsUnique_isBarycenter, MeasureTheory.IsBarycenter.mem_closedConvexHull, MeasureTheory.exists_isBarycenter_of_mem_closedConvexHull, MeasureTheory.mem_closedConvexHull_iff_exists_isBarycenter, MeasureTheory.isBarycenter_integral}
    \leanok

    In a Hausdorff real topological vector space, $x$ is the barycentre of a Borel probability
    measure $m$ if $\ell(x) = \int \ell\,dm$ for every continuous linear functional $\ell$. A
    probability measure carried by a compact convex $K$ has a barycentre in $K$, unique when the
    space is locally convex; for closed $C \subseteq K$, the points of the closed convex hull of
    $C$ are exactly the barycentres of probability measures carried by $C$. No metrizability is
    needed: Mathlib's compactness of the probability measures on a compact Hausdorff space
    replaces Georgii's passage to the metrizable $\mathcal C$-topology.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    Existence by Jensen's inequality for closed convex sets applied to finitely many functionals
    and the finite intersection property; uniqueness and the converse inclusion by the geometric
    Hahn--Banach theorem; the representation by Prokhorov compactness of the probability measures
    on $C$, the set of barycentres of such measures being closed, convex and containing $C$.
\end{proof}
