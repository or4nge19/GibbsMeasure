\section{Gibbs distributions with periodic boundary condition}

\begin{definition}[Elementary cubes, Georgii (17.12)--(17.15)]
    \label{def:rp-17-12}
    \uses{def:rp-17-7}
    \lean{MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.cubeCast, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.cubeView, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.cubeSiteRefl, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.cubeRefl, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.hatReflAt, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.hatReflection, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.hatPosAt, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.cubeView_hatReflection, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.cubeView_shift}
    \leanok

    $C = \{0,1\}^d$, $C(i) = C + i$ in $\Lambda = \mathbb Z_{2N}^d$, and the coarse-graining
    $\omega \mapsto \omega^* = (\omega_{C(i)})_{i \in \Lambda} \in (E^C)^\Lambda$. The reflection
    $r_k$ of the cube exchanges the two faces in direction $k$; the reflection $\hat r_k$ of the
    torus in the plane through the sites $\{i_k \in \{0, N\}\}$ is $i_k \mapsto -i_k$, with
    $\hat\Lambda_{+,k} = \{i_k \leq N\}$, and $(\hat r_k\omega)^* = \tilde r_k(\omega^*)$ where
    $\tilde r_k$ is the generalised reflection of $(E^C)^\Lambda$ built from $r_k$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Georgii (17.16)]
    \label{lem:rp-17-16}
    \uses{def:rp-17-12, def:rp-17-7}
    \lean{MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.isReflectionPositive_map_cubeView, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.measurePreserving_shift_map_cubeView, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.measurePreserving_genReflectionAt_map_cubeView, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.measurable_cubeView_cylinderEvents}
    \leanok

    If $\mu$ is $\hat r_k$-positive, resp.\ $\hat r_k$-invariant, resp.\ $\Lambda$-periodic, then
    its image $\mu^*$ under the coarse-graining is $\tilde r_k$-positive, resp.\ invariant, resp.\
    periodic.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    A function of the coordinates of $\omega^*$ in $\Lambda_{+,k}$ is a function of the
    coordinates of $\omega$ in $\hat\Lambda_{+,k}$, and $\tilde r_k \circ (\cdot)^* = (\cdot)^*
    \circ \hat r_k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Georgii (17.17)]
    \label{cor:rp-17-17}
    \uses{thm:rp-17-11, lem:rp-17-16}
    \lean{MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.abs_integral_prod_cubeView_pow_le}
    \leanok

    If $\mu$ is $\Lambda$-periodic and, for every $k$, $\hat r_k$-positive and $\hat r_k$-invariant,
    then for bounded measurable $f_i$ on $E^C$,
    $\bigl|\int \prod_i f_i(\omega_{C(i)})\,d\mu\bigr|^{|\Lambda|} \leq
    \prod_j \int \prod_i f_j(r^i\omega_{C(i)})\,d\mu$, with $r^i$ the iterated cube reflection.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    Theorem \ref{thm:rp-17-11} for $\mu^*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[$C$-potentials and periodic Gibbs distributions, Georgii (17.18)--(17.20)]
    \label{def:rp-17-18}
    \uses{def:rp-17-12}
    \lean{MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.periodicHamiltonian, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.periodicGibbs, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.periodicGibbsDist, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.periodicHamiltonian_shift, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.periodicHamiltonian_hatReflection, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.isFiniteMeasure_periodicGibbs, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.isProbabilityMeasure_periodicGibbsDist, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.measurePreserving_shift_periodicGibbs, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.measurePreserving_hatReflection_periodicGibbs}
    \leanok

    A $C$-potential is determined by one function $\Phi_C : E^C \to \mathbb R$; its periodic
    Hamiltonian on $\Lambda$ is $\sum_{i \in \Lambda} \Phi_C(\omega_{C(i)})$, and the Gibbs
    distribution with periodic boundary condition ${}^\circ\gamma_\Lambda^\Phi$ has density
    $e^{-\sum_i \Phi_C(\omega_{C(i)})}/{}^\circ Z$ relative to $\lambda^\Lambda$. It is a
    probability measure as soon as $\Phi_C$ is measurable and bounded below (the consequence of
    Georgii's condition (iv) that is used), $\Lambda$-periodic, and $\hat r_k$-invariant when
    $\Phi_C \circ r_k = \Phi_C$ (condition (iii)).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Georgii (17.21)]
    \label{thm:rp-17-21}
    \uses{def:rp-17-18, def:rp-17-7}
    \lean{MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.isReflectionPositive_hatReflection_pi, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.IsReflectionPositive.withDensity, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.IsReflectionPositive.smul, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.periodicHamiltonian_eq_add_hatReflection, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.isReflectionPositive_hatReflection_periodicGibbs, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.isReflectionPositive_hatReflection_periodicGibbsDist}
    \leanok

    For $\Phi_C$ measurable, bounded below and $r_k$-invariant, ${}^\circ\gamma_\Lambda^\Phi$ is
    $\hat r_k$-positive.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    $\lambda^\Lambda$ is $\hat r_k$-positive by Fubini, and reflection positivity passes to a
    density of the form $h \cdot h \circ \hat r_k$ with $h \in \mathcal A_{+,k}$; the density
    $e^{-H}$ has this form with $h = e^{-\sum_{C(i) \subseteq \hat\Lambda_{+,k}} \Phi_C(\omega_{C(i)})}$,
    every cube lying in one closed half or being the reflection of one in the other.
\end{proof}

    \label{cor:rp-17-17-gibbs}

    \leanok
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Georgii (17.26)]
    \label{lem:rp-17-26}
    \uses{def:rp-17-7}
    \lean{MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.torusPosAt_iff_torusReflAt_notMem, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.isReflectionPositive_siteEquiv_pi, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.isReflectionPositive_siteEquiv_withDensity, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.isReflectionPositive_siteEquiv_withDensity_sum, MeasureTheory.re_integral_mul_conj_comp_nonneg, MeasureTheory.re_integral_mul_conj_comp_pow_nonneg, MeasureTheory.re_integral_mul_conj_comp_exp_nonneg, MeasureTheory.integral_mul_comp_mul_exp_nonneg, MeasureTheory.integral_mul_comp_mul_exp_sum_nonneg}
    \leanok

    The reflection $r_k$ in a plane between the sites is the site permutation $i_k \mapsto
    -1 - i_k$, fixing no site. If a finite measure $\mu$ has a density relative to
    $\lambda^\Lambda$ of the form $\exp[h + h^* + \int m(dw)\, h_w h_w^*]$ with $m$ a finite
    measure, $h$ and $h_w$ complex, measurable (jointly in $w$), depending on the coordinates in
    $\Lambda_{+,k}$ and dominated by one measurable function of those coordinates, and
    $f^* = \overline{f \circ r_k}$, then $\mu$ is $r_k$-positive. In particular
    $\exp[h + h\circ r_k + \sum_a g_a \cdot g_a \circ r_k]$ with real bounded $h, g_a$ gives an
    $r_k$-positive measure, the shape of a nearest-neighbour model with nonnegative crossing
    couplings (Example (17.30)).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    Expanding the exponential and applying Fubini, $\mu(ff^*)$ is a series of terms
    $\lambda^\Lambda(gg^*) = |\lambda^{\Lambda_{+,k}}(g)|^2 \geq 0$; the truncation to
    $\{\varphi \leq c\}$ and dominated convergence handle the unbounded case.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Heisenberg-type potentials, Georgii (17.22)--(17.25)]
    \label{def:rp-17-22}
    \uses{def:rp-17-18}
    \lean{MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisenbergPotential, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisenbergPotential_pair, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.isPotential_heisenbergPotential, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisenbergExponent, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisenbergExponent_eq_offDiag_add_diag, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisenbergPeriodicGibbs, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisenbergPeriodicGibbsDist, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.periodizedCoupling}
    \leanok

    The pair potential $\Phi_{\{i,j\}}(\sigma) = -J(i-j)\,\sigma_i\cdot\sigma_j$ on
    $(\mathbb R^n)^{\mathbb Z^d}$ with $J$ even and summable (17.23); it is a $C$-potential only in
    the nearest-neighbour case, by (17.19)(2). The periodic Gibbs distribution (17.24) has density
    $\exp[\tfrac12\sum_{i,j\in\Lambda} J_\Lambda(i-j)\,\sigma_i\cdot\sigma_j]$ with the periodised
    coupling $J_\Lambda(i) = \sum_{j \equiv i} J(j)$ (17.25).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Georgii (17.24) as a theorem: the periodic modification of the Heisenberg potential]
    \label{thm:rp-17-24}
    \uses{def:rp-17-22, def:lo-G0}
    \lean{MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisBond, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisBond_injective, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.exists_eq_heisBond, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisenbergPotential_heisBond, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisBond_neg_self, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisBond_self_zero, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.lexPos, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.tsum_lexPos_eq_half, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.tsum_lexPos_intBoxLen_smul, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisenbergPeriodicPotential, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisenbergPeriodicPotential_pair, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisenbergPeriodicPotential_singleton, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisenbergPeriodicPotential_of_forall_ne, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.hasSummableRepSeries_heisenbergPotential, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.isPotential_heisenbergPeriodicPotential, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.interactingHamiltonian_heisenbergPeriodicPotential, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.map_periodicJuxt_heisenbergPeriodicGibbs, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisenbergPeriodicGibbsSpec, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisenbergPeriodicGibbsSpec_latticeBox, Potential.HasSummableRepSeries, Potential.HasSummableRepSeries.isPotential, Potential.periodicModification_sub_eq, Potential.isFiniteRange_periodicModification, Potential.eq_red_of_isRep_of_eq_translate, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.map_periodicJuxt_sigmaFiniteLambdaFun, Finset.pair_eq_pair_iff, MeasureTheory.map_comp_equiv_pi}
    \leanok

    The periodic modification $\tilde\Phi^{\Lambda(N)}$ of the Heisenberg potential (Example
    (4.20)(2) at the cube $\Lambda(N) = [-N,N)^d$ and the torus $(\mathbb Z/2N)^d$) has
    $\tilde\Phi_{\{i,j\}}(\sigma) = -J_\Lambda(i-j)\,\sigma_i\cdot\sigma_j$ for distinct sites of
    the cube, $\tilde\Phi_{\{i\}}(\sigma) = -\tfrac12 J_\Lambda(0)|\sigma_i|^2$ (the half is the
    two-to-one enumeration $r \leftrightarrow -r$ of the translates of a site), and vanishes on every
    other set; hence $H^{\tilde\Phi}_{\Lambda(N)} = -\tfrac12\sum_{i,j\in\Lambda}J_\Lambda(i-j)\,
    \sigma_i\cdot\sigma_j$, and the Gibbs kernel $\gamma^{\tilde\Phi}_{\Lambda(N)}(\cdot\mid\omega)$ is
    the image of the periodic Gibbs distribution ${}^\circ\gamma^\Phi_{\Lambda(N)}$ under the coupling
    with $\omega$, Georgii's ${}^\circ\gamma^\Phi_{\Lambda(N)} \times \delta_\omega$ of (5.20)(3), for
    a $\sigma$-finite a priori measure with finite partition function. The summability (17.23) is
    exactly the pointwise convergence of the representative series of (4.20)(2), which replaces the
    absolute summability (2.11) that the Heisenberg potential lacks; it is not needed off the
    diagonal, where the identity is an unconditional reindexing, and $J(0) = 0$ is used on the
    diagonal only. No erratum: the diagonal $\tfrac12 J_\Lambda(0)|\sigma_i|^2$ of (17.24) is confirmed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    Georgii's class $\mathcal R(\{i,j\})$ is enumerated faithfully by the relative period $r$
    (the translate of $\{i, j + 2Nr\}$ whose lexicographic anchor lies in the cube), and the
    interaction on the representative is $-J(i-j-2Nr)\,\sigma_i\cdot\sigma_j$; summing over $r$
    gives $J_\Lambda(i-j)$. At a single site the enumeration is two-to-one, whence the half.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Georgii (17.27)--(17.29)]

    \uses{def:rp-17-22, lem:rp-17-26}
    \lean{MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.repCharacter, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.IsNonnegDefiniteAt, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.IsNonnegDefiniteAt.mul, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.IsNonnegDefiniteAt.nonneg_of_odd, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.IsNonnegDefiniteAt.neg_one_zpow, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.IsNonnegDefiniteAt.posSemidef_crossingMatrix, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.heisenbergExponent_eq_add, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.isReflectionPositive_heisenbergPeriodicGibbs_of_posSemidef, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.isReflectionPositive_heisenbergPeriodicGibbs_of_isNonnegDefiniteAt, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.isReflectionPositive_heisenbergPeriodicGibbsDist_of_isNonnegDefiniteAt, Matrix.PosSemidef.exists_sum_mul, Summable.tendsto_boxAverage_sub}
    \leanok

    $J$ is nonnegative definite relative to $r_k$ if it has Georgii's representation (17.27),
    $J(i) = \int \alpha(dx)\,x^{i}$ by a measure $\alpha$ on the characters transverse to direction $k$,
    with the closure properties (17.28)(2)--(3) (products, odd coordinates, sign changes). Then
    (17.29): the periodic Gibbs distribution of a Heisenberg-type potential with such a $J$ is
    $r_k$-positive. The Berg--Maserick converse (17.28)(1), which Georgii cites without proof, is
    not formalised.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    \leanok
    The crossing part of the exponent, the bonds through the plane of $r_k$, has a coupling
    matrix which is a limit of nonnegative definite ones (Georgii's $L \to \infty$, taken here on
    the matrix rather than on the measures $\mu_{\Lambda,L}$); a Gram factorisation writes it as
    $\sum_a g_a\,g_a\circ r_k$, and Lemma \ref{lem:rp-17-26} in its finite-sum form applies.
\end{proof}

    \leanok

    Nearest-neighbour couplings give $r_k$-positive periodic Gibbs distributions iff $J(e_k) \geq 0$
    (17.30; the sufficiency needs only $J(e_k) \geq 0$, the coupling may be antiferromagnetic
    transverse to $k$), next-nearest-neighbour couplings are nonnegative definite relative to $r_k$
    iff $A \geq 2(d-1)|B|$ and $r_k$-positive iff so (17.31, both directions as theorems), and
    $J(i) = |i|^{-a}$ is nonnegative definite on $\mathbb Z$ for $a > 0$ and on $\mathbb Z^2$ for
    $a > 1$, giving $r_k$-positive periodic Gibbs distributions for the summable cases $a > 1$ on
    $\mathbb Z$ and $a > 2$ on $\mathbb Z^2$ (17.32). The Heisenberg potential is provably not
    absolutely summable in the sense of (2.11), so the periodic Gibbs distribution (17.24) is
    defined here by its density and identified with the Gibbs distribution of the periodic
    modification in Theorem \ref{thm:rp-17-24}. The necessity halves are Georgii's evaluation of ${}^\circ Z\,{}^\circ\gamma(ff^*) =
    4e^{S}\sinh\langle q, Mq\rangle$ at a $\pm 1$ pattern $q$ for the two-point a priori measure,
    with $M$ the crossing matrix; the $d = 2$ case of (17.32) represents $(k^2 + \ell^2)^{-a/2}$
    through the Cauchy kernel, whose transform is the two-sided exponential, and a Beta-type
    mixing integral. Three errata: the constant in (17.30) is $2(2N)^{d-1}$, not $(2N)^{d-1}$
    (two interfaces meet the plane of $r_k$ on the torus); the displayed middle expression there
    is $4\cosh$, not $4\sinh$ (the factor $\sigma_u\sigma_{r_k u}$ is missing), the final value
    being right; and in (17.31) Georgii's second test function $\tilde f$, which alternates along
    $k$ as our copy of the text prints it (the subscript may be an OCR error), gives exactly the
    same value as $f$ for every next-nearest-neighbour coupling, because
    crossing bonds join sites with equal $k$-coordinates --- it must alternate in the directions
    transverse to $k$, and with that reading his argument is correct. Not formalised: the
    Berg--Maserick converse (17.28)(1), which the book cites without proof.
\end{example}
