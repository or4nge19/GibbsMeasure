\section{Spitzer's example of totally broken shift-invariance}

\begin{theorem}[Georgii (11.43)--(11.47)]
    \label{thm:sbi-11-43}
    \uses{def:bl-transfer-matrix, thm:bl-11-14}
    \lean{MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Markov.Spitzer.step, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Markov.Spitzer.noImmigration, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Markov.Spitzer.noImmigration_pow_apply_singleton, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Markov.Spitzer.step_pow_apply_singleton, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Markov.Spitzer.sum_firstPassage_step, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Markov.Spitzer.map_shift_ne, MeasureTheory.GibbsMeasure.Markov.Spitzer.infinite_extremePoints_G}
    \leanok

    For Georgii's subcritical branching matrix with immigration at extinction, the powers of the
    no-immigration part are binomial, $\ell(x, p^n, y)$ (11.45), and the first-passage
    decomposition (11.47) expresses $P^n(x,y)$ through the taboo powers and the hitting times of a
    state; the extinction times satisfy $\sum_{m<n} f_m(x) = (1-p^n)^x$ for $x \geq 1$. If in
    addition no change of measure of $Q = P^2$ is positive recurrent, then every shift moves the
    Gibbs measures and $\operatorname{ex}\mathcal G(\gamma^Q)$ is infinite; that null recurrence is
    a hypothesis here, not a theorem, so (11.46) itself is not formalised.
\end{theorem}
